## Key Families & ATC Additions This Session

### Deck 19 — Antihypertensives (לחץ דם)
- 16 families organized by mechanism:
  - **β-Blockers** — 3 sub-families: non-selective (β1+β2), cardioselective (β1), partial agonists (ISA)
  - **α/β Blockers** — Labetalol (pregnancy/emergency), Carvedilol (CHF)
  - **α1-Blockers** — Prazosin, Doxazosin, Terazosin (not first-line)
  - **RAAS inhibitors** — ACE Inhibitors (10 ingredients), ARBs (8 ingredients), Direct Renin Inhibitor (Aliskiren), Aldosterone Antagonists (Spironolactone)
  - **CCBs** — 3 sub-families: DHP (6 ingredients, vascular), Phenylalkylamines (Verapamil, heart+vessels), Benzothiazepines (Diltiazem, heart+vessels)
  - **Centrally acting α2-agonists** — Clonidine (resistant HTN), Methyldopa (pregnancy)
  - **Direct vasodilators** — Hydralazine (gestational HTN), Minoxidil (also alopecia)
- **ATC additions:**
  - **ARNI** — Sacubitril/Valsartan (Entresto, 2015) — new class, HFrEF + HTN
  - **Non-steroidal MRA** — Finerenone (Kerendia, 2021) — CKD+T2DM
- **Cross-ref (blue row):** Diuretics — mentioned in overview slide 21 but detailed in deck 20
- Pindolol intentionally appears in both F01 (non-selective) and F03 (partial agonist) — dual classification

### Deck 20 — Diuretics (משתנים)
- 8 families organized by nephron site of action:
  - **Carbonic Anhydrase Inhibitors** (PCT) — Acetazolamide; weak, short-lived
  - **Osmotic Diuretics** (descending loop) — Mannitol, Urea; IV only, acute use
  - **Loop Diuretics** (TAL) — Furosemide, Bumetanide, Torsemide, Ethacrynic acid; most efficacious (high ceiling)
  - **Thiazide Diuretics** (DCT) — HCTZ, Chlorthalidone, Metolazone, Indapamide; most common for HTN (low ceiling)
  - **K⁺-Sparing: Na⁺ Channel Inhibitors** (collecting tubule) — Triamterene, Amiloride
  - **K⁺-Sparing: Aldosterone Antagonists** (collecting tubule) — Spironolactone, Eplerenone
  - **ADH Antagonists (Vaptans)** (collecting duct) — Tolvaptan; pure aquaresis
- **ATC addition:**
  - **SGLT2 Inhibitors** — Dapagliflozin (2014), Empagliflozin (2014), Canagliflozin (2013) — osmotic diuretic mechanism; primarily diabetes/HF/CKD drugs (will also appear in endocrine deck)
- **Unique columns in Families sheet:** `Na_excretion`, `K_effect`, `body_pH` — key differentiators between diuretic classes (matches the electrolyte summary table from slide 25)
